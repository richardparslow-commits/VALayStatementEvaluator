# Drafting Guide for VA Lay / Witness Statements

Use this guide when drafting new statements. Every draft must be factual, first-person,
grounded in the provided medical records and the witness's own observations, and must never
invent facts. Where the records contain information the witness has not personally confirmed,
mark it as a bracketed placeholder for the witness to verify, e.g., "[Confirm: records show
physical therapy starting March 2021 — did you drive him to those sessions?]"

## Required structure (VA Form 21-10210 style)

1. **Header** — RE: Statement in Support of Claim for [Veteran Name] — [Condition]. Note the
   statement is submitted on/with VA Form 21-10210 (Lay/Witness Statement).
2. **Introduction & credentials of observation** — who the witness is, relationship to the
   veteran, how long they have known each other, and the witness's opportunity to observe
   (lives with / worked with / served with / sees weekly). Sample opening: "I am writing to
   support [Veteran] in [his/her] claim for VA disability benefits for [condition]. I have
   known [him/her] since [year] as [his/her] [relationship]. I have personally observed the
   following."
3. **In-service event / onset (if witness has personal knowledge)** — what the witness saw,
   with date or time frame, place, unit/location, who else was present. If the witness did not
   witness the in-service event (most family witnesses), say so honestly and anchor instead on
   what they observed at the veteran's return or first contact.
4. **Observed symptoms and their progression** — organized by theme or chronology. Every
   symptom claim needs: what specifically was observed, how often, since when, and a concrete
   example. Use the transformation: weak "He has trouble sleeping" → strong "Since his 2020
   deployment, he wakes multiple times a night from nightmares, often pacing the house for an
   hour before returning to bed."
5. **Functional impact** — separate concrete descriptions of impact on (a) work, (b) daily
   living, (c) family/social relationships. Quantify where the records or witness knowledge
   allow (missed workdays, activities abandoned, social events avoided).
6. **Continuity statement** — affirm whether symptoms have been continuous since the onset
   period; honestly acknowledge any known gaps and their reasons.
7. **Closing & certification** — reaffirm truthfulness: "I certify that this statement is true
   and correct to the best of my knowledge and belief." Then signature line, printed name,
   date, phone/email (VA Form 21-10210 Sections IV–V).

## Style rules

- First person, plain language, short declarative sentences. No legalese, no medical jargon
  beyond naming the claimed condition.
- Facts, not conclusions: replace "he is disabled" with the observed limitations that show it.
- No medical opinions: never diagnose, never assert causation. Frame everything as observation
  ("I have seen", "He told me", "On three occasions I watched").
- Specific dates/times wherever known; honest approximations otherwise ("around 2018",
  "several times a week"). Never fabricate precision.
- No exaggeration, melodrama, or accusatory language. Understatement is safer than
  overstatement; adjudicators weigh plausibility and internal consistency.
- Distinguish personal observation from what the veteran reported: "I personally saw..." vs.
  "He told me that...". Both are legitimate; labels build credibility.
- One condition focus per statement; if supporting multiple conditions, VA prefers separate
  forms per issue/witness.

## Grounding against medical records (mandatory step)

Before drafting:
1. Build the medical facts digest from the uploaded records.
2. Match every record-derived fact the draft will use (dates, diagnoses, treatments,
   facilities) to a specific record entry; cite it in the grounding notes.
3. Where the witness's observations align with records, note the corroboration — this is the
   strongest form of lay evidence.
4. Where the witness reports something not in the records, keep it (lay evidence need not be
   corroborated — Buchanan/Barr) but flag it in the grounding notes so the witness can double
   check their memory before signing.
5. Where records conflict with the witness's account, DO NOT silently pick one side — surface
   the conflict to the user and draft only what the witness can truthfully support.
6. Surface omissions: record facts that could strengthen the statement (e.g., documented pain
   ratings, failed treatments, hospitalizations) as bracketed questions for the witness.

## Claim-type adaptations

- **Service connection**: emphasize in-service event (if known) + onset + continuity.
- **Increased rating**: emphasize worsening over time, compare past vs. present function,
  reference recent treatment if known.
- **PTSD stressor**: detailed event narrative (time, place, unit, who was present, what
  happened, immediate aftermath); a fellow service member's corroboration is gold.
- **TDIU/unemployability**: observed work limitations, missed days, job loss circumstances,
  inability to perform specific job tasks.
