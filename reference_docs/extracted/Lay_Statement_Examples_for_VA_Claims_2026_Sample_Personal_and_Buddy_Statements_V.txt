Example: Back Injury (Personal)
"This statement supports my claim for a lower back condition. In August 1996, while
stationed at Fort Example, I injured my back lifting equipment during a field exercise. I was
treated at the aid station and returned to duty without a formal record. Since then I have
had daily lower back pain. Today I cannot stand for more than 20 minutes, I wake up at
night from the pain, and I had to leave a warehouse job because I could not lift. I certify this
is true to the best of my knowledge." Why it works: specific date and place, the undocumented
event, and concrete present-day impact.

Example: PTSD Stressor (Personal)
"This statement supports my claim for PTSD. During a 2008 deployment, our position came
under attack and I witnessed a fellow soldier seriously injured. Since returning, I have
trouble sleeping, avoid crowds, and startle easily at loud noises. I have distanced myself
from family and struggle at work when I feel closed in." Why it works: names the stressor, then
describes specific, ongoing symptoms and their impact. Share only what you are comfortable
writing.

Example: Buddy Statement, Injury
"I served with Mike in the same unit at Fort Example in 1996. I personally saw him injure
his back lifting equipment during a field exercise that summer. He was in clear pain and
could barely straighten up for days afterward. In the years since, I have seen him struggle
with lifting and avoid activities he used to do easily. I certify this is true to the best of my
knowledge." Why it works: establishes the relationship, describes a witnessed event, and notes
lasting impact.

Example: Continuity of Symptoms
"This statement addresses the continuity of my knee condition. I first hurt my knee in
service in 2001 and have had pain and swelling ever since. I did not seek treatment right
away because I did not want to be pulled from duty, and after discharge I managed it on my
own until it worsened. The problem has been continuous from service to today." Why it
works: it bridges a treatment gap and shows the symptom has persisted since service.

What form should a lay statement go on?
A personal statement written by the veteran can go on VA Form 21-4138, the Statement in Support of
Claim, or VA Form 21-10210, the Lay/Witness Statement. A buddy statement written by someone else
should go on Form 21-10210, which since 2021 is the VA's preferred form for lay and witness statements,
with a separate form for each witness. A statement can also be written in a word processor, signed, and
uploaded to your claim.
